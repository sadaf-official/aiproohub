## Why Expired Listings Are Still Listing Gold

Expired sellers are the warmest cold prospects in real estate. Unlike FSBO sellers (see our [FSBO outreach guide](/blog/art-08-fsbo-outreach-ai-without-spammy.html)), expired sellers already wanted an agent. The relationship failed, not the motivation. That makes them skeptical but highly responsive to the agent who shows up differently.

The math favors you. Expireds convert to relists at rates far above other prospecting sources, because the seller's need is confirmed and urgent. Most agents prospect expireds for a week and quit. The ones who run a real system build a listing pipeline competitors never see.

Your edge is specificity. Other agents say "I can sell your home." You say "your home sat 127 days priced 8 percent above the three comparable sales on your street, and the photos never showed the renovated kitchen." One of those gets the callback.

**The expired opportunity scorer**

> "Score this expired listing as a prospect. Facts: [address, price history, days on market, beds/baths, expired date]. Market: [3 comparable sales]. Output: a 1-10 opportunity score with reasoning, the most likely reason it did not sell, and the strongest first-outreach angle."

Why it works: it forces you to lead with the property's real story and tells you which expireds deserve your time first.

## Build a Clean Expired List With AI

A campaign is only as good as its list. Pull expireds and withdrawn listings from your MLS for your farm area, going back 90 to 180 days. AI cleans and prioritizes: dedupes addresses, flags data errors, and sorts by signals like price reductions and long days on market.

Respect the compliance lines. Many expired sellers are on the Do Not Call registry, and TCPA and state rules restrict calls and texts without consent [VERIFY]. Mail and door knocking remain the reliable channels, and your CRM should tag every contact's do-not-call status before you dial.

Enrich each record with what outreach needs: price history, photo quality, one personal detail from public records. AI turns this into a one-line brief per property, so you personalize 30 mailers in the time five used to take.

**The expired list enricher**

> "Turn this expired listing data into outreach-ready briefs. For each property: [paste MLS rows]. Output: a one-line summary, the price history as a timeline, and the most notable fact for personalization. Table format, no fluff."

Why it works: it converts a spreadsheet into personalized talking points, the difference between "Dear Homeowner" and a mailer that reads like you studied their home.

## Lead With Data: The Value Angle That Gets Callbacks

Expired sellers have heard every promise. What they have not heard is an honest diagnosis backed by numbers: the answer to why their home did not sell.

Build the angle from three data points: list price versus comparable sales, marketing quality versus competing listings (photos, description, virtual tour), and market movement during the listing period. AI assembles the narrative; you verify every number.

Never attack the previous agent. Frame everything as market facts: "the three closest sales closed at $412,000 to $428,000 while the listing held at $465,000." Facts persuade without you sounding like a vulture.

**The data-driven value angle**

> "Build my value angle for this expired listing. Data: [address, price history, DOM, 3 comparable sales, photo count vs competing listings]. Output: a 3-sentence diagnosis stated as market facts with zero criticism of the prior agent, then 3 bullets of what I would do differently. Confident, respectful tone."

Why it works: the "no criticism" constraint keeps you credible. Sellers trust the agent who diagnoses without trash-talking.

## Run the Five-Touch Expired Sequence

One call does not win expireds. The agents who convert run a sequence across channels over two to three weeks.

- **Touch 1, day 0:** the diagnostic mailer (next section). Mail first, because it arrives with your full case made.
- **Touch 2, day 3:** a short call referencing the mailer, or a 30-second voicemail.
- **Touch 3, day 5:** a text, only with a compliant path [VERIFY]. Otherwise a second, shorter mailer.
- **Touch 4, day 8:** the door knock with the diagnostic report in hand.
- **Touch 5, day 14:** a final "closing the file" message that often gets the reply the first four did not.

Track every touch in your CRM. Expired campaigns compound: this month's sequence feeds next month's relist follow-up list.

**The five-touch sequence planner**

> "Plan a five-touch expired listing outreach sequence for: [address, seller name, key diagnosis, my phone]. For each touch: day, channel, the script or copy (calls under 75 words, mailers under 120), and the goal. Include a compliant opt-out line for texts [VERIFY]. Professional tone."

Why it works: it produces the whole campaign in one pass, so you execute instead of improvising.

## Mailers Expired Sellers Actually Keep

Expired sellers receive agent mail daily and recycle most of it. Yours survives by looking nothing like the others: a plain one-page letter that opens with their property's data and closes with one question.

The structure: line one names their home and its listing history, paragraph two gives the diagnosis in two sentences, paragraph three lists what you would do differently in three bullets, and the close asks one question: "Would a 15-minute conversation about the numbers be useful?" Pair strong mailers with our [direct mail guide](/blog/art-25-direct-mail-postcards-ai-copy.html).

Hand-address the envelope. It gets opened at multiples of the rate of metered bulk mail.

**The expired diagnostic letter**

> "Write a one-page letter to an expired listing seller. Facts: [seller name, address, listing history, my 2-sentence diagnosis, 3 bullets of what I would do differently, my name and phone]. Rules: open with their property's data, no self-promotion, no criticism of the prior agent, no promises about speed or price. Close with one question offering a 15-minute numbers conversation. Under 250 words."

Why it works: the data-first opening proves you did homework, and the single-question close is easy to say yes to.

## Door-Knock Scripts That Open Doors

The door knock is the highest-converting expired touch and the one agents avoid most: a calm, brief visit with something valuable in hand beats ten cold calls.

You have 30 seconds before the door closes. Lead with value, not your name: "I put together a one-page breakdown of why homes on this street are selling and yours did not get its shot. Can I leave it with you?" You are offering analysis, not asking for the listing. Most sellers take the paper, and the paper does the selling.

AI preps the knock: a 60-second briefing per door with the property facts, the diagnosis, and the one question to ask. Knock mid-morning or late afternoon, never at dinner. If nobody is home, leave the report with a handwritten sticky note.

**The door-knock briefing**

> "Write a door-knock briefing for this expired listing: [address, seller name, price history, my diagnosis in 2 sentences]. Include: a 30-second opening script (value first, name second), 3 questions if they engage, 2 graceful exits if they are busy, and a 20-word sticky note if nobody answers. Calm, respectful, zero pressure."

Why it works: it removes the improvisation that makes door knocking terrifying.

## Follow Up After the No and Win the Relist

Most expireds do not convert on the first campaign. They convert three to nine months later, when the seller remembers exactly one agent: the one who brought the diagnostic report and never pressured them. Your follow-up system is where expired prospecting pays.

Build a relist watch list in your CRM: every expired contacted, with the date, the diagnosis shared, and the response. Send quarterly touches: a street market update, a nearby sold comparable, a brief check-in.

AI drafts each quarterly touch from fresh data in minutes. When the seller is ready, you are the only call they make.

**The relist nurture writer**

> "Write a quarterly follow-up for an expired seller I contacted [X] months ago. Context: [address, my original diagnosis, their response, one new market development]. Under 100 words. Reference my original diagnosis, share the new development, end with a soft open door. No pressure, no filler."

Why it works: referencing your original diagnosis proves you were right and consistent, exactly what a burned seller needs before trusting an agent again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How soon after a listing expires can I contact the seller?**
Check your MLS rules and state regulations first; some restrict solicitation of expireds for a set period [VERIFY]. Where allowed, mail can go out within days. Calls and texts must respect Do Not Call and TCPA rules regardless.

**What if the seller relisted with another agent already?**
Back off immediately and note it in your CRM. Contacting a seller under an active listing agreement can violate MLS and state rules [VERIFY]. Add them to a future watch list; listings expire again.

**How is expired prospecting different from FSBO prospecting?**
FSBO sellers chose to sell alone, so you prove an agent's value. Expired sellers already believe in agents; you prove you are different from the one who failed. Diagnosis for expireds, value proof for FSBOs.

**Should I offer a lower commission to win an expired listing?**
Leading with a commission cut signals desperation. Win on your diagnosis and plan first, then negotiate from demonstrated value.

**How many expireds should I work at once?**
Twenty to thirty active prospects is the sweet spot: enough for a real pipeline, few enough that every mailer, call, and knock gets genuine preparation. AI research makes 30 manageable.
