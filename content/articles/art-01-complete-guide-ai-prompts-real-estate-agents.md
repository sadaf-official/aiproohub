## What Is an AI Prompt (and Why Should Agents Care)?

An AI prompt is simply the instruction you give an AI tool like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. The quality of what comes out is determined almost entirely by the quality of what goes in.

Most agents prompt like this:

> "Write a listing description for a 4-bedroom house."

And they get back something generic, robotic, and vaguely embarrassing — the kind of copy that starts with "Welcome to this stunning..." and ends with nobody calling.

Top-producing agents prompt like this:

> "You are an experienced luxury listing copywriter. Write a 180-word MLS description for 123 Maple Street, a 4-bed/3-bath Craftsman built in 1925. Key features: original millwork, wraparound porch, chef's kitchen renovated 2023, walkable to downtown. Tone: warm and story-driven, never salesy. Open with a sensory detail. Describe the home, never the people (fair housing compliant). End with a soft call to action for a private showing."

Same AI. Radically different output. The difference isn't the tool — it's the prompt. (For a detailed breakdown of which AI tool fits which task, see our [ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini for Realtors](/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini-for-realtors.html) comparison.)

## The Anatomy of a Perfect Real Estate Prompt

Every high-performing real estate prompt contains five elements. Memorize this framework — it's the foundation of everything that follows:

**1. Role.** Tell the AI who to be. "You are a luxury listing copywriter" produces different output than "you are a helpful assistant." Specific roles unlock specific expertise.

**2. Facts.** Feed it the raw material: address, beds, baths, square footage, standout features, neighborhood details, price positioning. AI can't invent your listing — it can only elevate what you give it.

**3. Format.** Specify length, structure, and platform. "180-word MLS description" vs. "Instagram caption under 150 words with hashtags" vs. "3-email follow-up sequence." Format constraints are where generic becomes usable.

**4. Voice.** Describe the tone: warm, confident, conversational, luxury-polished. Better yet, paste a sample of your own writing and ask the AI to match it. (Our full [voice-training method](/blog/art-11-make-ai-sound-like-you-voice-training.html) takes three minutes and fixes the "robotic AI" problem permanently.)

**5. Guardrails.** State the rules: fair-housing compliant, describe the property not the people, no invented features, verify all facts. Guardrails are what separate professional AI use from risky AI use — and they're covered in depth in our [fair-housing-safe AI listing copy guide](/blog/art-02-fair-housing-safe-ai-listing-copy.html).

**Role + Facts + Format + Voice + Guardrails.** Five elements, thirty seconds to write, and the output jumps from generic to genuinely good.

## Fair Housing: The Non-Negotiable Guardrails

This is the section that matters most. AI doesn't know fair-housing law — and its training data contains decades of biased real estate copy. Every prompt you use must include compliance guardrails, and every output must pass human review.

The core rules:

1. **Describe the property, never the people.** "Chef's kitchen with quartz counters" ✓. "Perfect for young professionals" ✗. "Walkable to downtown shops" ✓. "Near great churches" ✗ (steers by religion).
2. **Never mention protected classes** — race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin (plus state-level additions like source of income).
3. **Screen every output.** Run AI copy through a fair-housing checklist before publishing. Our prompts build this screening into the workflow itself.
4. **Humans review everything.** AI drafts, you approve. No exceptions. This is the rule that keeps your license safe.

The complete compliance system — risky phrase lists, safe rewrites, screening prompts, and state-by-state notes — is in [Fair-Housing-Safe AI Listing Copy](/blog/art-02-fair-housing-safe-ai-listing-copy.html). Read it before you publish a single AI-assisted word.

## 7 Mistakes Agents Make With AI (and How to Avoid Them)

**1. Vague prompts.** "Write me a listing description" produces vague copy. Specificity in, specificity out. Use the 5-element anatomy above.

**2. Publishing without review.** AI hallucinates facts — square footage, school names, distances. Verify every factual claim against your MLS sheet. The 60-second review checklist catches 99% of issues.

**3. Accepting the first draft.** First drafts are starting points. Iterate: "warmer," "shorter," "lead with the kitchen." Refinement is where good becomes great.

**4. Skipping voice training.** Without it, your copy sounds like everyone else's AI copy. Three minutes of voice training fixes this permanently ([method here](/blog/art-11-make-ai-sound-like-you-voice-training.html)).

**5. Ignoring compliance.** Covered above. The mistake with legal consequences.

**6. Tool-hopping.** ChatGPT this week, a new app next week, mastery never. Pick one tool, learn it deeply, systematize. (Our [honest tool comparison](/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini-for-realtors.html) helps you pick once, correctly.)

**7. Quitting after one bad output.** One bad output is a bad prompt, not a bad tool. Troubleshoot, adjust, retry. The agents winning with AI are the ones who persisted past week two.

Each mistake gets the full treatment in [AI Mistakes Real Estate Agents Make](/blog/art-29-ai-mistakes-real-estate-agents-make.html).
