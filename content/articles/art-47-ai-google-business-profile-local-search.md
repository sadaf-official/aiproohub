## Why the Map Pack Decides Your Local Market

Google's map pack is the block of three business listings with a map above organic results for local searches like "realtor near me" or "listing agent in [city]." Most local searchers contact a business straight from the results, and for real estate the profile panel gets the click, because clients want a phone number, hours, and review count in one glance.

Here is the part agents miss: the map pack is winnable. Google ranks profiles on three factors, relevance, distance, and prominence. You cannot change your office location, but relevance and prominence are in your hands. Relevance comes from a complete profile: categories, services, description, and attributes that match what people search. Prominence comes from reviews, review responses, posts, photos, and engagement. Every one of those inputs is something AI can help you produce. ## Set Up the Profile Right the First Time

Most agent profiles are 60% complete. Here is the full checklist, with where AI saves you time.

**Business name.** Use your real, registered business name, exactly as clients know it. Google actively penalizes and sometimes suspends profiles with keyword-stuffed names, and competitors report them. More below.

**Primary category.** For most agents this is "Real Estate Agent," the strongest relevance signal, so get it right. Add secondaries you genuinely qualify for, like "Real Estate Consultant," but never stack categories for things you do not do.

**Services.** List every service: buyer representation, seller representation, relocation assistance, first-time buyer programs, investment consulting, home valuation. Each service has a description field, which is where AI saves you the most time.

**Service areas.** If you do not have a public storefront, set your profile to service-area mode and list the cities, towns, and neighborhoods you actually serve. Listing areas you have never closed in helps nothing, but the suburbs and neighborhoods you genuinely cover should all be named.

**Hours, phone, website.** Fill everything, including holiday hours. A profile with no hours looks abandoned.

**Attributes.** Select what applies: women-led, veteran-led, and languages spoken (Spanish, Mandarin, and others matter in diverse markets).

Use this prompt to draft the services section:

> "I am a real estate agent in [city, state]. My services are: [list services, e.g., buyer representation, seller representation, relocation, investment consulting, home valuation]. Write one 2-sentence Google Business Profile service description for each service. Tone: professional, warm, plain English. Include the city name naturally in about half of them. No hype words like best, top-rated, or number one."

Why it works: services descriptions are tedious by hand, so agents leave them blank. This fills every one in a single pass, with location terms distributed naturally instead of stuffed.

## Let AI Write Your Business Description

The business description allows 750 characters. Most agents write a flat paragraph that could describe anyone. Yours should tell Google what you are and where you work, tell a human why you are worth calling, and read like a person wrote it.

Structure it in three moves: who you are (agent or team plus city), what you do (specialties plus one differentiator), and an invitation (call for a free consultation). Work your market neighborhoods into the second sentence, naturally.

> "Write a 750-character Google Business Profile business description for me: I am [name/team], a real estate agent in [city, state] serving [2-4 neighborhoods or nearby towns]. I specialize in [e.g., first-time buyers and listing homes under $500k]. My differentiator: [e.g., former appraiser / 15 years in this zip code / fluent Spanish]. Include a call to action to call or message for a free consultation. Warm, professional tone, no clichés like dream home or stunning."

Why it works: the prompt forces the three-move structure, the location coverage, and the differentiator, all inside the character limit. ## Win Reviews and Respond to Every One

Reviews are the biggest prominence factor you control, and for agents they double as conversion proof. A profile with 60 recent five-star reviews ranks better and converts the click into a call. Google also weights recency: ten fresh reviews beat fifty from three years ago.

**Getting reviews.** Ask the day after closing, when the client is happiest. Use the "Share review form" link in your profile for a direct link. A short text is enough:

> "Write a short, warm text message I can send to clients the day after closing, asking them to leave me a Google review. It should: thank them, include a placeholder for my review link, say it takes 60 seconds, and mention that specific details about their experience help future buyers. Keep it under 60 words. No pressure tone."

Why it works: agents skip review asks because writing them feels awkward. This removes the friction, and the "specific details" line nudges clients toward keyword-rich reviews that mention buying, selling, and your market.

**Responding to every review.** Review responses are a ranking factor, and buyers read them. Respond to all of them, including the one-stars. Keep it short, human, and specific: mention the client by name and reference something real from the review.

AI drafts these well, with one rule: never paste a response without personalizing it. A generic "Thank you for your kind review!" repeated forty times reads like automation.

> "Draft a response to this 5-star Google review from my real estate client. Review: [paste the review]. Address them by name, reference one specific thing they mentioned, keep it to 2-3 sentences, and end with a warm sign-off. Do not use exclamation points or the phrase 'kind review.'"

Why it works: the banned-phrase instruction keeps forty responses from sounding identical.

For the review you dread, the one-star, never respond hot. Sleep on it, then use a calm template:

> "Draft a professional, calm public response to a 1-star Google review for my real estate business. The complaint: [summarize]. Do not argue, assign blame, or offer legal detail. Take responsibility where reasonable, state my commitment to making it right, and invite the reviewer to call me directly at [phone] to resolve it. Keep it under 80 words. The audience is not the reviewer, it is every future client reading this thread."

Why it works: most agents ignore bad reviews or respond defensively. This produces the response that protects you: composed, accountable, and offering a real conversation.

## Posts, Q&A, and Photos: Staying Fresh

Google rewards profiles that show signs of life. Three features keep yours fresh: posts, Q&A, and photos.

**Google Business Profile posts.** Posts appear on your profile and in the map pack, but fade from prominent display after about a week, so monthly (ideally weekly) posting keeps you looking active. Good topics: a new listing going live, an open house, a market stat, a client success story, or a seasonal tip:

> "Write a Google Business Profile post for my real estate business: [topic, e.g., open house this Saturday 12-3pm at address]. Include the address, one detail that makes the home worth visiting, and a call to action to RSVP or just drop by. Under 100 words, friendly tone, no emojis overload."

Why it works: GBP posts are short-lived, so they should be fast to produce. This bakes in the address, the hook, and the CTA.

**Q&A.** Anyone can ask questions on your profile, and anyone can answer, so a stranger can define you if you do not. Seed the section with the questions buyers and sellers actually ask ("Do you work with first-time buyers?", "What areas do you serve?", "Do you speak Spanish?"), then answer them from your business account.

**Photos.** Profiles with photos get far more engagement. For agents: a professional headshot, your office, recent sold properties (with client permission), neighborhood shots, community events. Skip stock photos; buyers spot them. Upload a few each month.

## How Your Profile Feeds AI Search Assistants

When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini "find me a good realtor in [city]," the answer often draws on structured local business data, and Google Business Profile is one of the primary sources. The completeness work above is also AI-search optimization: a profile with a detailed services list, location-rich description, dozens of substantive reviews, and seeded Q&A gives AI assistants far more to cite you with than a bare-bones listing. Thin profiles are invisible twice over: once in the map pack, once in AI answers.

One caution: AI assistants read your reviews too. Glowing reviews with thoughtful owner responses read as trustworthy when a model summarizes "reputable agents in [city]."

## Mistakes That Quietly Kill Your Profile

**Keyword stuffing the business name.** "Jane Smith Realtor | Best Agent in Austin | Homes for Sale" looks tempting. Google explicitly forbids it, competitors report it, and suspension means your profile disappears while you appeal. Your real name, nothing more.

**The half-finished profile.** No hours, no services, no description, three photos from 2021. Complete profiles rank better and get more clicks, and an incomplete one signals to buyers that you are not detail oriented.

**Fake or incentivized reviews.** Buying reviews violates Google's policy and can get the profile suspended. When the filter catches it, your review count collapses publicly.

**Ignoring the Q&A section.** If you do not seed and monitor it, someone else writes your story. Check it monthly.

**Duplicated or outdated info.** If your phone, hours, or address disagree across your profile, website, Zillow, and Realtor.com, Google trusts you less. Keep your name, address, and phone consistent everywhere.

**Set and forget.** The agents dominating your market's map pack did not set up their profile once in 2019. They post, upload photos, answer reviews, and seed Q&A continuously. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take for Google Business Profile changes to affect rankings?**
Edits usually go live within days, but ranking movement typically takes two to six weeks. Reviews and posts move the needle faster because they signal active engagement.

**Can real estate agents have more than one Google Business Profile?**
Generally no, one profile per business location. Solo agents working from home can run a service-area profile. Agents on a team should not each create duplicates of the same business; that risks suspension.

**Do Google reviews really affect my map pack ranking?**
Yes. Count, average rating, recency, and the keywords clients use in reviews all feed into prominence, one of Google's three local ranking factors.

**Should I respond to old reviews too?**
Yes, especially negative ones. A gracious reply to a two-year-old complaint can still convert a reader today.

**Is keyword stuffing my business name worth the risk?**
No. Google forbids it, competitors report it, and a suspension removes your profile from search during the appeal. Put keywords in the description, services, and posts instead.
